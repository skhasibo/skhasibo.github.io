Proton movements are essential for Cu(I) transfer from metallochaperones to the Cys-X-X-Cys motifs of Cu-ATPases, yet the molecular events that initiate this process remain poorly understood. In particular, whether the incoming cysteine coordinates Cu(I) as a thiol or thiolate, and how this initial coordination evolves toward productive metal transfer, remain unresolved. Here, we address these questions using a multiscale computational approach combining DFT-based pK a calculations, classical molecular dynamics simulations, and QM/MM molecular dynamics simulations. Our results reveal several key findings. First, under aqueous conditions, Cu(I) coordination proceeds preferen-tially through direct binding of the neutral thiol, as thiol deprotonation presents a significant energetic barrier relative to Cu-S bond formation. Furthermore, no significant pK a shift is observed for the incoming cysteine in either the isolated protein or the initial holo-apo encounter complex, indicating that the early stage of Cu(I) transfer is governed by protein-protein interactions that facilitate ligand approach rather than intrinsic cysteine pKa modulation. The subsequent evolution of the transient three-coordinate Cu(I)-thiolate intermediate was investigated using QM/MM and longer-timescale classical molecular dynamics simulations, revealing Cu(I)- induced structural remodeling of the protein environment that generates conformational states conducive to productive metal transfer. This work provides a revised mechanistic framework for biological Cu(I) trafficking in which neutral thiol coordination initiates Cu(I) transfer, while subsequent Cu(I)-induced protein structural rearrangements enable productive metal exchange.
