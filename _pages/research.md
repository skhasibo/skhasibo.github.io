My research lies at the intersection of **Computational Biophysics** and **Bioinorganic Chemistry**, where I investigate how protein environments control metal ion recognition, selectivity, and transport. Using multiscale modeling approaches—ranging from high-level quantum mechanical calculations to long-timescale molecular dynamics simulations—I aim to uncover the molecular principles governing copper trafficking in biological systems.

        <h3 class="archive__item-title">2. Role of Cysteine Protonation and Solvation in Metal Transfer</h3>
        <div class="archive__item-excerpt">
          <p>A central question in copper trafficking is whether the cysteine ligands are protonated (thiol) or deprotonated (thiolate) during the initial encounter. Our work has shown that explicit solvation is non-negotiable for accurate modeling; ignoring the water shell leads to massive errors in reaction energetics. I’ve demonstrated that Cu(I) transfer can occur via mechanisms that don't require the traditional deprotonation step previously assumed in the literature.</p>

        <h3 class="archive__item-title">3. Conformational Pre-organization in Metallochaperone-Mediated Copper Transfer</h3>
        <div class="archive__item-excerpt">
          <p>In a collaborative study on the Atx1–Ccc2a copper transfer system, I performed the QM/MM electronic structure calculations to characterize the Cu(I) coordination environment and reaction energetics along representative conformational states identified from MD simulations. My calculations quantified the relative stabilization of thiolate-bound Cu(I) species and clarified the electronic factors governing metal transfer. These results complemented the kinetic modeling and provided a mechanistic basis for understanding copper hand-off in this system.</p>

### Key Methodologies
* **Quantum Chemistry:** DFT, pKa calculations, and QM/MM for reaction pathways.
* **Classical MD:** Solvent effects, protein-protein docking, and membrane protein simulations.
* **Statistical Mechanics:** Markov State Models (MSM) for long-time kinetics and free energy landscapes.
